The First World War ran from 1914 to 1918, with Britain, France, Russia and later the United States against Germany and its allies. At sea it was a war between two big battle fleets that met in force only once, fought around them by raiders, submarines and mines. The stories here cover the fleets, the raiders and the strange ships that came out of it.

## The dreadnought race

The naval side of the war started before 1914. In 1906 [HMS Dreadnought](/stories/hms-dreadnought-hoax/) made every battleship afloat obsolete: heavy armour, ten 12-inch guns and steam turbines. Britain and Germany spent the next eight years building fleets around her design. Dreadnought herself never fired on an enemy battleship. Her only kill came in 1915, when her bow cut the submarine U-29 in two.

The Royal Navy also tried to make its submarines keep up with the battle fleet. The answer was steam. The [K-class submarines](/stories/k-class-kalamity/) made 24 knots on the surface and needed five minutes to dive. None were sunk by the enemy. Six sank anyway, several in collisions with their own fleet.

## Raiders, U-boats and the war on trade

Germany's overseas cruisers went after merchant shipping from the first weeks. [SMS Emden captured or sank over twenty ships in the Indian Ocean](/stories/emden-penang-raid/), then rigged a fake fourth funnel, slipped into Penang in October 1914 and torpedoed the Russian cruiser Zhemchug at anchor while her captain was ashore at a hotel.

The U-boat became the bigger weapon. On 7 May 1915, U-20 fired one torpedo into the liner [Lusitania off the south coast of Ireland](/stories/lusitania-sinking/). Survivors felt two explosions and she was gone in eighteen minutes. The arguments about the warning notice, the cargo and the second blast are still running.

Small ships fought in the narrow seas. In the Channel in late 1916, one Tribal-class destroyer lost her bow to a torpedo and another lost her stern to a mine. The Royal Navy joined the halves and sent [the result, HMS Zubian, back to the Dover Patrol](/stories/hms-zubian-frankenship/), where she sank a U-boat. The United States entered the war in 1917, and in March 1918 the collier [USS Cyclops vanished between Barbados and Baltimore with 306 people](/stories/uss-cyclops-vanished/). No wreck has been found.

## Jutland

The one big fleet action came on 31 May 1916. At [Jutland](/stories/battle-of-jutland/), three British battlecruisers exploded and took more than 3,300 men with them. Beatty's flagship Lion took the same kind of hit and survived because of a dying Royal Marine and a warrant officer who had enforced the magazine rules. [HMS Warspite's helm jammed](/stories/hms-warspite-grand-old-lady/) and she circled twice in front of the German High Seas Fleet, then lived to fight through the next war too. The German fleet went home and never again risked a full fleet action.

## Revolution and endings

Russia's navy came into the war already damaged. The cruiser [Aurora had been shot at by her own squadron off Dogger Bank in 1904](/stories/aurora-friendly-fire/), survived Tsushima, and in 1917 her crew mutinied. On the evening of 25 October 1917 she fired a single blank round on the Neva, which Soviet lore made the signal for the assault on the Winter Palace.

Germany's fleet ended the war interned at Scapa Flow. On 21 June 1919 its own crews opened the valves, and [52 warships sank in a British anchorage](/stories/scapa-flow-scuttling/) while a boatload of schoolchildren watched.

The United States closed its war with a ceremony. In 1921 the old cruiser [USS Olympia, Dewey's flagship at Manila Bay, carried the Unknown Soldier home from France](/stories/uss-olympia-unknown-soldier/) and nearly lost him to an Atlantic storm on the way.
