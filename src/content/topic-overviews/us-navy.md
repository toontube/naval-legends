The United States Navy grew from six frigates paid for in 1794 into the largest fleet in the world by the end of the Second World War. Along the way it fought the Barbary corsairs, Britain, the Confederacy, Spain, Germany and Japan, and then spent the Cold War watching the Soviet Union. The stories here follow its ships through those wars and through the accidents in between.

## Sail, iron and steel

The Naval Act of 1794 paid for six frigates, designed by Joshua Humphreys to outgun anything that could catch them and outrun anything that could outgun them. [USS Constitution](/stories/uss-constitution-old-ironsides/) beat HMS Guerriere in 1812, became "Old Ironsides", and is still in commission at Boston.

In the Civil War the Navy fought its own former ship. The Confederates rebuilt the burned frigate Merrimack as the ironclad CSS Virginia, and on 9 March 1862 [USS Monitor fought her to a draw at Hampton Roads](/stories/monitor-vs-virginia-ironclads/), the first battle between ironclads.

By 1898 the Navy was steel and coal. The protected cruiser USS Olympia was Dewey's flagship at Manila Bay, and in 1921 she [carried the Unknown Soldier home from France through a hurricane](/stories/uss-olympia-unknown-soldier/). In the First World War the collier [USS Cyclops vanished in 1918 with 306 people aboard](/stories/uss-cyclops-vanished/). She is still the largest loss of life in the Navy's history not caused by combat.

## Pearl Harbor and the Pacific war

The Second World War began for the US Navy on 7 December 1941. [USS Arizona lost 1,177 men](/stories/uss-arizona-pearl-harbor/), close to half the American dead that day. [USS Nevada was the only battleship to get underway](/stories/uss-nevada-pearl-harbor/), and her crew beached her to keep the channel open. On [USS New Orleans, with no power, the crew passed shells to the guns by hand](/stories/uss-new-orleans-pearl-harbor/) while her chaplain urged them on. [USS Phoenix steamed out untouched](/stories/uss-phoenix-belgrano/).

Six months later codebreakers at Pearl Harbor [tricked Japan into confirming that its target "AF" was Midway](/stories/battle-of-midway/), and Japan lost four carriers. [USS Enterprise](/stories/uss-enterprise-cv6/) earned 20 battle stars, more than any other American warship, and was sold for scrap in 1958 anyway.

Off Guadalcanal, more than fifty ships from both sides sank in [Ironbottom Sound](/stories/ironbottom-sound/). New Orleans lost 150 feet of hull to one torpedo and [sailed home backwards behind a bow of coconut logs](/stories/uss-new-orleans-coconut-bow/). At [Leyte Gulf in 1944](/stories/battle-of-leyte-gulf/), Halsey took the bait and the escort carriers and destroyers of Taffy 3 faced Yamato's force off Samar.

In July 1945 [USS Indianapolis delivered the uranium for the Hiroshima bomb](/stories/uss-indianapolis-sharks/) and was torpedoed four days later. About 900 men went into the water and 316 survived.

## Ships with problems

Not every story is about combat. The cruiser [USS Wichita needed 200 tons of pig iron in her bottom](/stories/uss-wichita-pig-iron/) to stay stable. The [Cleveland-class cruisers](/stories/cleveland-class-top-heavy/), the largest cruiser class ever built, grew so top-heavy with anti-aircraft guns that equipment had to come off to make room for more. In 1943 the destroyer [USS William D. Porter fired a live torpedo at the battleship carrying President Roosevelt](/stories/uss-porter-torpedo-fdr/).

## The Cold War

After 1945 Nevada was painted orange and used as a target for an atomic bomb at Bikini. Most of the losses that followed were peacetime ones. In 1952 [USS Hobson turned into the path of the carrier Wasp](/stories/uss-hobson-wasp-collision/) at night and 176 men died. In 1968 North Korea captured the spy ship [USS Pueblo](/stories/uss-pueblo-captured/), which is still held in Pyongyang. Phoenix, sold to Argentina as General Belgrano, was sunk by a British submarine in 1982.
