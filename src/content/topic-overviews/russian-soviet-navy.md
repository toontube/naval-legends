Russia's navy has been three navies in a little over a century: the Imperial fleet of the Tsars, the Soviet Navy, and the Russian Navy that inherited Soviet ships after 1991. It has usually been split between separate seas, the Baltic, the Black Sea, the Pacific and the Arctic, with long distances between them. Several of its worst days came from that geography, and from a habit of keeping bad news quiet. The stories here run from 1904 to 2022.

## The Tsar's fleet and Tsushima

In 1904 Russia was at war with Japan, and what was left of its Pacific ships sat at Port Arthur. In October, Admiral Rozhestvensky sailed from the Baltic with the Second Pacific Squadron on an 18,000-mile voyage to relieve them. Days out, in fog off Dogger Bank, the squadron took British fishing trawlers from Hull for Japanese torpedo boats and opened fire. The trawler Crane was sunk, and [the cruiser Aurora was hit five times by her own battleships](/stories/aurora-friendly-fire/).

The fleet sailed on for seven months. At [Tsushima on 27 and 28 May 1905](/stories/battle-of-tsushima/), Admiral Togo destroyed it for the loss of three torpedo boats. On the second morning Rear Admiral Nebogatov, surrounded with the last Russian battleships, hoisted surrender signals. A handful of cruisers, Aurora among them, got away to Manila and were interned.

## War and revolution

In the First World War the Russian cruiser Zhemchug was in Penang in October 1914, boilers cold and no lookouts posted, while her captain was at a hotel ashore. [The German raider Emden came in with a fake fourth funnel and torpedoed her at anchor](/stories/emden-penang-raid/). Eighty-one of her crew died.

In 1917 the navy turned on its officers. Aurora's crew mutinied in February and shot their captain. On the evening of 25 October she fired a single blank round on the Neva, which Soviet lore made the signal for the assault on the Winter Palace. She opened as a museum ship in 1957 and is still in St Petersburg.

## The Soviet Navy

In the Second World War the Soviet submarine S-13, under Alexander Marinesko, [sank the German refugee ship Wilhelm Gustloff](/stories/wilhelm-gustloff/) in the Baltic on 30 January 1945. It was the deadliest ship sinking ever recorded. Marinesko was demoted, discharged and jailed, and only made a Hero of the Soviet Union in 1990.

During the Cold War the Soviet Navy built ships to kill American carriers, and at Mykolaiv it launched a carrier in late 1988, officially a "heavy aircraft-carrying cruiser" so she could pass the Turkish Straits. Renamed Varyag, she was 68 percent complete when the Soviet Union ended in 1991. Ukraine could not afford to finish her. [A Chinese front man bought the hull for $20 million as a casino](/stories/varyag-casino-carrier/), and in 2012 she was commissioned as China's first aircraft carrier, Liaoning.

## The Russian Navy after 1991

On 12 August 2000, in the Barents Sea, a leaking practice torpedo [sank the submarine Kursk with 118 men](/stories/kursk-submarine-disaster/). Twenty-three survived the explosions. Russia refused foreign help for days, and Norwegian divers opened the hatch nine days late.

The cruiser [Moskva](/stories/moskva-sinking-2022/), the Black Sea Fleet flagship, was one of the Slava class built at Mykolaiv to hunt carriers. In April 2022, during Russia's invasion of Ukraine, two Ukrainian Neptune missiles hit her off Odesa and she sank on 14 April. Moscow admitted one death. Its own court later counted 20.
