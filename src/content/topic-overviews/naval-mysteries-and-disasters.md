Most warships that are lost are lost in battle, and the record says how. This hub is about the others: ships that sank with huge numbers of people aboard, ships that went down through bad design or bad luck, and ships that left port and were never found. Some of these cases are well documented. Some are still open, and the stories say plainly where the evidence stops.

## The deadliest sinkings

The worst naval disasters are not the famous ones. Our [ranked list of the worst naval disasters by death toll](/stories/worst-naval-disasters/) starts with a ship most people have never heard of. On 30 January 1945 the Soviet submarine S-13 torpedoed the former cruise liner [Wilhelm Gustloff](/stories/wilhelm-gustloff/) in the Baltic. She had been built for about 1,900 people and carried somewhere around 10,000, most of them refugees. Estimates of the dead run from 6,000 to over 9,000, far more than the Titanic.

Many of the worst losses came in single moments. [HMS Hood blew up in the Denmark Strait in May 1941](/stories/hms-hood-three-survivors/), and three of her 1,418 men survived. At Pearl Harbor, [USS Arizona lost 1,177 men](/stories/uss-arizona-pearl-harbor/), close to half of all the Americans killed that day. Others came slowly. After [USS Indianapolis was torpedoed in July 1945](/stories/uss-indianapolis-sharks/), about 900 men went into the water and 316 survived.

The [Lusitania](/stories/lusitania-sinking/) sank in eighteen minutes in 1915 after one torpedo. Survivors felt two explosions, and the arguments about what caused the second one are sorted in the story into what is known and what is not.

## Ships that sank themselves

Some ships needed no enemy. On 19 July 1545 [the Mary Rose heeled and flooded through her open gunports](/stories/mary-rose-solent-1545/) in front of Henry VIII. In 1628 Sweden's [Vasa failed a stability test at the dock, sailed anyway](/stories/vasa-maiden-voyage/), and sank after about 1,300 meters. Nobody was blamed.

In 1944 the Japanese carrier [Shinano, the largest yet built, sank seventeen hours into her first voyage](/stories/shinano-maiden-voyage/) after one submarine attack, with 1,435 dead. Most of her watertight doors had never been installed.

Navies have also been their own worst enemy at sea. The Royal Navy's steam-powered [K-class submarines](/stories/k-class-kalamity/) lost six boats, none of them to the enemy. In 1952 the destroyer-minesweeper [USS Hobson turned across the bow of the carrier USS Wasp](/stories/uss-hobson-wasp-collision/) at night and was cut in half. 176 men died, and the captain did not survive to explain the turn. In August 2000, [a leaking practice torpedo sank the Russian submarine Kursk with 118 men](/stories/kursk-submarine-disaster/), and the government's handling of the rescue became a second disaster on television.

## Ships that disappeared

Then there are the ships with no wreck at all. In March 1918 the collier [USS Cyclops left Barbados with 306 people and 11,000 tons of manganese ore](/stories/uss-cyclops-vanished/), more than her recommended load. No distress signal was received and no wreckage was found. It is still the largest non-combat loss of life in US Navy history.

Cyclops is not alone. Our list of [ships that disappeared without a trace](/stories/ships-that-disappeared/) covers her alongside the liner SS Waratah in 1909, the MV Joyita in 1955, found drifting with nobody aboard, and the submarine USS Scorpion in 1968. Each story sets out what was found, what was not, and which theories the evidence supports.
